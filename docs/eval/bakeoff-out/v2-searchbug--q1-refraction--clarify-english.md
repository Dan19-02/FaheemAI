Light travels at different speeds in different materials, and this sudden change in speed is what makes a straight pencil look bent at the boundary of water.

Imagine a shopping cart rolling from a thick carpet onto a smooth tiled floor at an angle. The wheel hitting the tile first speeds up, while the other wheel is still moving slowly on the carpet, forcing the cart to pivot and change direction.

Similarly, light rays coming from the underwater pencil speed up as they exit into the air, bending away from the normal. Because our brain assumes light only travels in straight lines, it traces these bent rays straight back, making the underwater pencil appear shallower than it actually is.

**Exam Edge:** When drawing ray diagrams for this phenomenon, a common slip is bending the light rays the wrong way at the boundary. Always remember that light bends away from the normal line when it speeds up as it exits the denser water into the rarer air.

If you look at a coin placed at the bottom of a bucket of water, would it appear deeper, shallower, or at its actual depth based on how light bends here?
