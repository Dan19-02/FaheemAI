# Faheem vs. Fable 5 — Blind Arabic Head-to-Head

**Question:** on real Arabic curriculum questions, does the *deployed Faheem product*
(its own answer engine) beat handing a Bahraini student a raw frontier model?

**Answer, honestly: not yet.** Raw **Fable 5** (a frontier Claude model) outscored the
live Faheem pipeline **8.96 to 8.61** overall and took **35 of 60** best-answer votes.
Faheem still wins the dimension it is built for (**pedagogy**) and wins decisively with
the student who needs it most (the struggling grade-9 learner), but its **underlying
generation model (Gemini 3.5 Flash) is outclassed on raw accuracy, language, and depth.**

> ### ⚠️ Read this first — what this test is, and isn't
> - **Faheem = the REAL product**, not Claude-in-a-costume. Every Faheem answer was
>   generated by the running backend `/api/chat` pipeline on **Gemini 3.5 Flash**, with
>   the full Arabic teaching spec, auto-routing, and the deep-verify examiner pass
>   (all 10 answers passed verify). This is what a Bahraini student actually receives.
> - **Fable 5 = raw frontier model**, given a neutral generic-assistant prompt in Arabic
>   (no teaching doctrine, no product spec) — the "just paste it into a chatbot" baseline.
> - **Judges & students are AI personas** (3 Bahrain teacher personas, 3 Bahrain student
>   personas), not real licensed teachers or children.
> - **Blind & rotated.** Each judge read anonymized answers (أ) / (ب); which system sat
>   in which slot was rotated per question. No system self-identified.
> - **Validate before external claims.** These numbers show relative quality and a
>   working method; re-run with **real Bahraini students and teachers** before marketing.

---

## Method

**10 Arabic questions** (Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics; grades 9–12; mix of
numericals, concept "why", comparison, a stressed-student plea, and a misconception
probe) × **2 systems** = 20 answers, each scored by **6 judges** = **120 blind
answer-scores across 60 head-to-head gradings.**

Rubric, integer 1–10 per dimension: **Accuracy · Curriculum fit · Arabic language ·
Pedagogy · Trust.** Each judge also picked the better answer per question.

---

## Results — the head-to-head

| System | Overall | Accuracy | Curriculum | Language | Pedagogy | Trust | Best-answer wins |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 🥇 **Fable 5** (raw) | **8.96** | **9.65** | **8.98** | **8.88** | 8.45 | **8.83** | **35 / 60** |
| 🥈 **Faheem** (product, Gemini Flash) | 8.61 | 9.10 | 8.50 | 8.42 | **8.68** | 8.33 | 25 / 60 |

**Fable 5 leads by +0.35 overall.** Faheem's only dimension win is **pedagogy (+0.23)** —
the teaching layer is real, but it does not close the base-model gap on accuracy (−0.55),
trust (−0.50), curriculum (−0.48), and language (−0.46).

### Who preferred whom

| Judge group | Faheem | Fable 5 | Best-answer wins |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Teachers (3) | 8.71 | **9.12** | Faheem 10 / Fable **20** |
| Students (3) | 8.50 | **8.80** | Faheem **15** / Fable 15 |

The split is clean and believable:
- **Teachers preferred Fable's crisp, exam-shaped answers** (definition → numbered steps
  → units → independent verification), and penalized Faheem's occasional verbosity.
- **Students split evenly** — and the *struggling grade-9 student preferred Faheem 8–2*,
  citing its Gulf-localized analogies ("علبة الحلوى البحرينية", "عداد السرعة بشارع خليفة
  بن سلمان", "سوق المنامة") and short, patient sentences.

## Why Faheem won where it won (the product layer is real)

Judge notes credit Faheem, blind, for exactly the things the spec adds:
- **Misconception correction as content.** On mitosis/meiosis (Q3) Faheem corrected the
  classic "DNA replicates twice in meiosis" error (it replicates once); on photosynthesis
  (Q8) it corrected "plants don't respire." Teachers T2/T3 and student S2 rewarded this.
- **Mechanism-bearing, Bahrain-localized analogies.** The candy-box for the mole (Q7) and
  the Manama-souk / speedometer analogies landed with the weaker students where Fable's
  cleaner prose did not reach them.
- **Follow-up practice questions and exam-trap warnings** ("watch the sign", "don't
  multiply the oxygen wrong"), which read as teaching, not just answering.
- **Warm, staged relief** for the anxious numerical (Q1's "10 → 20 → 30" build).

## Why Faheem lost where it lost (the honest gaps)

1. **The base model is weaker.** Gemini 3.5 Flash simply trails a frontier Claude on raw
   accuracy and depth. On Faraday (Q5) and the derivative (Q9), Fable gave fuller,
   better-organized physics (flux `Φ = BA cosθ`, three ways to change it, Lenz as energy
   conservation) that Faheem's answer only gestured at.
2. **A real citation-leak bug.** Faheem's Q5 answer contained **12 stray `[1]` markers
   with zero attached sources** — Gemini's search-grounding citation markers that never
   resolved to source chips, so raw `[1]` leaked into the student-facing text. **Every
   judge** flagged it ("مراجع [1] وهمية", "shattered my trust"), and it sank Q5 (8.00) and
   dragged Faheem's trust score. This is fixable: strip unresolved `[n]` markers in the
   same backstop that strips role-labels.
3. **The stressed-student answer (Q6) misfired, 0–6.** Faheem read "laws of motion" and
   labelled the *kinematic equations* as "Newton's first/second/third law" — a term slip
   the judges caught, while Fable both fixed the wording **and** answered the real plea
   (active recall, spaced repetition, sleep, write the laws in the exam margin).
4. **Occasional verbosity** and one Arabic spelling slip ("جديتان") cost small margins
   with the exam-minded teachers and the time-pressed anxious student.

---

## What this means

- **The Faheem product layer adds measurable value** (pedagogy win, misconception
  correction, localization, the struggling-student win) — but on this run it is **carried
  by a generation model that a frontier competitor now beats.** That is precisely why the
  architecture kept generation **provider-agnostic behind an adapter (decision D1)**: this
  result is the strongest evidence yet to **re-run the model bake-off and consider moving
  the generation tier up** (a frontier model behind the same Faheem spec would plausibly
  reclaim the accuracy/language/trust gap while keeping the pedagogy edge).
- **Fix the `[1]` citation-leak** before any external demo — it is a cheap, high-visibility
  trust win.
- **Numericals and misconception-heavy concepts are Faheem's current strength**; deep
  derivations and exam-shaped model answers are where the base-model gap shows most.
