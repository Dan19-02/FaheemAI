Light travels at different speeds depending on the medium it is passing through, just like how you would slow down if you were running on a dry road and suddenly stepped into deep water. When the light rays coming from the pencil leave the water and enter the air, they speed up instantly, which causes the rays to bend. Our brain is programmed to think light always travels in a perfectly straight line, so it perceives the pencil as being in a slightly different position than where it actually is, creating that "bent" look at the water surface.

This concept was introduced in your Class 10 chapter 'Light: Reflection and Refraction' and you will encounter it in detail during Class 12 Ray Optics.
