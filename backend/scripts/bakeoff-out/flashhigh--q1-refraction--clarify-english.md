When light travels from water into air to reach your eyes, it speeds up and bends, tricking your brain into seeing the pencil in the wrong spot. This concept of refraction was introduced in the Class 10 Science chapter 'Light: Reflection and Refraction' and returns in Class 12 'Ray Optics'.

Think of a toy car rolling from a smooth floor onto a thick carpet at an angle. The wheel that hits the carpet first slows down, causing the car to pivot and change direction. 

Similarly, water is denser than air, so light travels slower in it. When light reflecting off the underwater pencil exits into the air, it speeds up and bends away from the normal. Your brain assumes light always travels in straight lines, so it traces these bent rays straight back, making the underwater pencil appear shifted upward.

A common trap in exam questions is mixing up the direction of bending: remember that light bends *away* from the normal when escaping a denser medium like water into a rarer medium like air, not toward it.

If you replace the water with a much denser liquid like glycerin, where light travels even slower, would the pencil appear more bent or less bent?
