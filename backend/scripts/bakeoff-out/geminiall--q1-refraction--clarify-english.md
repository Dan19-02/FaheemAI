When light travels from water into air, it speeds up and bends, which tricks our eyes into seeing the submerged part of the pencil in a slightly shifted position [Physics: Refraction of Light · Class 10]. 

To understand why it bends, imagine pushing a shopping cart from a smooth concrete floor onto a patch of grass at an angle. The moment the front-left wheel hits the grass, it slows down, while the other wheels are still moving fast on the concrete. This sudden difference in speed forces the cart to pivot and change direction. Similarly, light travels slower in water than in air [Physics: Refraction of Light · Class 10]. When light rays leaving the pencil hit the boundary between water and air at an angle, they speed up and bend [Physics: Refraction of Light · Class 10]. 

Our brain always assumes that light travels in a perfectly straight line. When these bent rays reach our eyes, our brain traces them straight back, making the underwater part of the pencil appear slightly higher and shifted compared to the dry part above [Physics: Refraction of Light · Class 10]. This topic was introduced in the Class 10 Science chapter 'Light: Reflection and Refraction' and will return in Class 12 'Ray Optics and Optical Instruments' [Physics: Refraction in the CBSE/NCERT syllabus · CBSE · Class 10].

Students often make the mistake in ray diagrams of drawing light rays traveling from our eyes to the pencil. Remember, to see the pencil, light must travel from the pencil, bend at the water-air boundary, and then enter our eyes.
